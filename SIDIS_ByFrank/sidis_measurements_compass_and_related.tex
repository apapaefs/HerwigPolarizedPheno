\begin{abstract}
This note collects the principal publication-level measurements of semi-inclusive deep-inelastic
scattering (SIDIS) from COMPASS and the most directly comparable fixed-target programs:
HERMES and Jefferson Lab (Hall A, Hall B/CLAS and CLAS12, and Hall C).  Selected legacy
measurements from SMC, EMC, and Fermilab E665 are included for historical and phenomenological
context.  The scope is ordinary SIDIS,
\[
  \ell N\rightarrow \ell' h X ,
\]
including single-hadron, identified-hadron, dihadron, and hyperon observables.  Exclusive vector
meson production and DVCS are intentionally excluded.  Conference proceedings and preliminary
analyses are generally not listed when a final refereed publication is available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and conventions}

The catalog is organized by the observable actually measured rather than by the parton distribution
or fragmentation function subsequently extracted in phenomenology.  Typical categories are
unpolarized multiplicities and cross sections, transverse-momentum spectra, azimuthal modulations,
longitudinal-spin observables, transverse-target TMD asymmetries, dihadron interference-fragmentation
observables, and hyperon polarization.

Two points are important for current phenomenology:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The 2026 COMPASS addendum to the isoscalar charged-pion, charged-kaon, and unidentified-hadron
multiplicities uses updated radiative corrections and explicitly supersedes the corresponding
2017 multiplicity data sets \cite{COMPASS:addendum2026,COMPASS:pionmult2017,COMPASS:kaonmult2017}.
\item The new 2022-data COMPASS deuteron dihadron result was accepted by \emph{Physical Review
Letters} in June 2026.  At the date of this note the stable citation is therefore the arXiv record
and APS DOI rather than an invented volume/page assignment \cite{COMPASS:dihadronD2026}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{COMPASS}

COMPASS combines high-energy muon beams with proton and deuteron targets and consequently spans
unpolarized SIDIS, longitudinal spin, transverse-target TMD observables, dihadron transversity, and
hyperon polarization.

\subsection{Transverse-target TMD asymmetries}

For a transversely polarized target, the measured SIDIS cross section contains five target
single-spin and three beam--target double-spin modulations.  Among the leading-twist channels are

 A_{UT}^{\sin(\phi_h+\phi_S)} \quad\text{(Collins)},

\section{Jefferson Lab}

Jefferson Lab is complementary to COMPASS/HERMES because it emphasizes the valence region at lower
beam energy and typically larger $x$.  Hall A provides neutron-sensitive $^3$He data, Hall B
(CLASS/CLAS12) provides large acceptance and multidimensional angular measurements, and Hall C
provides high-precision cross sections and multiplicities.

\section{Boundary with non-SIDIS measurements}

Belle, BaBar, and BESIII fragmentation measurements are essential complementary constraints on
fragmentation functions, but they are $e^+e^-$ annihilation rather than SIDIS and are therefore not
listed here.  Likewise, exclusive $\rho$, $\phi$, DVCS, and other exclusive channels from COMPASS,
HERMES, or Jefferson Lab are excluded even when their event selections share part of the DIS
kinematic domain.

\section{Notes on completeness}

This is intended as a publication-level map of the principal fixed-target SIDIS data sets through
27 August 2026, not a catalog of every conference proceeding, thesis, detector note, preliminary
binning release, or later re-analysis of an already published event sample.  A truly machine-ready
global-fit inventory should additionally record, for each data release, the exact bin boundaries,
kinematic cuts, covariance treatment, radiative corrections, acceptance conventions, and the
location of HEPData or collaboration tables.
